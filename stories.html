Field notes

One place. One change. Written short.

01

Sake axis

When the queue became the clock

Mornings ran on jerrycans until the gravity line cut the walk.

Above the Sake market, the day started with container math and who could be spared for the path. The spring line was a second square—news and late school arrivals both began there.

After the protected spring and market tap, the farthest compounds gained back part of the morning. Commercial and domestic users still compete, so the association posted hours instead of hoping for goodwill.

Meetings could start nearer the agreed time because fewer members were still on the path. Progress measured in punctuality, not speeches.

HopeCongo funded capital and a first fittings kit. Queue law stays local; inspectors check flow and the spare box.

02

Kiwanja

Four pumps, one service map

Scattered compounds shared a failure mode the committee could log.

Kiwanja is tracks, not a grid. Private bends and favours failed in the rains. Public pumps made load-sharing possible.

A September entry that swapped one seal was deliberately dull. Dull means the map still works.

Households still own roofs and latrines. The village gained a shared asset and a log format.

Field staff read the log before they praise pump handles against the sky.

03

Masisi

Fence before praise

Cattle and drinkers cannot share an open spring head forever.

Paths crossed at the source for years. Blame moved faster than wire. Protection put posts in before any gravity-line celebration.

A cut strand is logged like a leak. Villages without a fence still walk farther for clearer water.

Commons stay common only when something physical blocks the easiest abuse.

Refusing to fund an unprotected source is respect for village time and money.

04

Corridor

Agendas, not only alarms

Steady assets let secretaries post dates instead of shouting after every break.

Many villages met only in crisis. Panic attendance is not planning.

With systems running, agendas appeared in advance. Repair votes could wait for quorum.

HopeCongo observes when invited; it does not chair.

Fee discipline still varies. Notes refuse a uniform corridor myth.

05

Nyiragongo

The yard is the noticeboard

Along the volcano road, the school compound is where word travels.

Dust, Goma traffic, compounds that double as landmarks. WASH on the yard changes who lingers after class.

Soap rotas at the gate leak outward when younger siblings copy what they see.

One block does not redesign a mountain slope. It strengthens a node the village already treats as central.

A mid-year rota that is still real means the node holds without permanent NGO staff.

06

Walungu

Evening traffic, old roof

Lamps pulled a thin stream toward a room that already existed.

The literacy circle borrowed community space. Dry-season evenings saw children and guardians where paths had gone quiet after dusk.

Attendance measures trust as much as pedagogy.

The scarce input is facilitators, not concrete.

Walungu stays on the education ledger so a no-building project is still counted.

07

After launch

Quiet months are the test

Ownership shows when nobody is filming.

The test is a quiet month: fees still collected, log still written, spare seal still in the box.

Committees that pass ask for a specific part or a second training slot—not another ceremony.

Field packs prefer inspection dates over launch photos.

Ownership is boring. Boring is the goal.

08

Water routes

Two fewer trips

When children carried water, school competed with thirst.

Mothers and older sisters listed the routine without decoration. Girls lost more opening lessons when water ranked first.

Nearer taps cut repeat trips. Teachers saw fewer late marks—not a miracle enrolment spike.

Family gain is time and less wear on feet.

Interviews stay short. Families do not owe biographies for a pump.

09

Minova

After the break

Empty stomachs were a practical reason to leave.

Staff could predict who faded after noon. Term meals lowered the cost of staying for margin households.

Some families still choose chores or trade.

Stock against the roll stops silent expansion past budget.

Success is presence after noon, not the first-ladle photo.

10

Filters

Cracked pot, intact habit

Cleaning survived hardware failure.

Users demonstrated rinsing without prompts. Finished units meant missing hardware, not abandoned treatment.

Swap-outs reconnect habit to a working pot.

Logs note who actually rinses.

Public packs use counts, not addresses, unless consent says otherwise.

11

Shasha teachers

Pay that keeps the room open

Stipends can stop mid-year teacher exit.

Irregular pay pushes local teachers toward Goma trade. Pupils inherit rotation or an empty room.

Sample weeks and dual signatures exist because pressure invents ghost names.

The teacher’s family feels stability first; pupils feel continuity.

Narrow support—not a silent district payroll.

12

Dawn queue

Seniority is strategy

Posted hours need someone who will say no at opening.

Tactics appear fast: place-holding, child-planted jerrycans, cash for commercial priority.

Locked pumps as revenge punish everyone, including fee-payers.

Steel is easy. Dawn enforcement is scarce.

A fair line is progress before any ledger.

13

School gate

Same tippy-tap for adults

Skipping parents produced clean hands at school only.

A demo in the pickup window beats a workshop only the free can attend.

School-year soap is for the block; household bars are family cost.

Later visits ask if soap remains and if a guardian still recognises the tippy-tap.

Short shared instructions travel farther than pamphlets.

14

Remittances

Money changes the line

Better-off draws can crowd domestic users.

Some associations try commercial windows; others lean on women’s group pressure.

The job is keeping the asset usable for fee-paying domestic users.

Inequality does not vanish at the stand. Design can stop it from breaking the stand.

Honesty about this is part of family-centred notes.

15

Compounds

Three households, one path

Internal turns meet public queue rules at the gate.

Extended compounds negotiate who walks when before they reach the public stand.

Conflicts at the tap sometimes started at home.

Interviews map compound size when it explains use.

Design that pretends every household is nuclear misreads the line.

16

Hard months

Empty pot, full social cost

Fair fees can still hurt in a bad harvest.

Short arrears without cutting water keep legitimacy. Locking taps for small debts pushes families to unsafe sources.

Field advice favours graduated responses.

Family dignity is part of whether a point remains a public good.

Capital budgets rarely price this social load; notes should.

17

Shasha

Desks with the roofing plan

Fit-out booked so rooms could not sit empty a term.

Sixty desks match three rooms without a phantom second shift.

Procurement and construction shared a milestone.

First ordinary shortage was chalk, not structure.

Cost sits in the education share of deployed funds.

18

Walungu

Catch-up, then exit

Lost months need drills—and a path back to the timetable.

Facilitators work in the classroom language inside existing space.

Kits wear out; replacements are budgeted.

Sample-week averages beat lifetime stacks that never remove anyone.

A defined education-page share keeps the circle visible.

19

Nyiragongo

Eight stalls, living rota

Hand-over meant who sweeps and who reports a broken tap.

Hardware without a rota fails by month three.

Mid-year checks look at soap and latches, not inauguration photos.

Contractors got safeguarding briefings before tools came out.

Keys transferred is a completion test.

20

Controls

Ghosts fail a second signature

Scarce support needs rules teachers can respect.

Dual signatories and presence samples exist because pressure invents beneficiaries.

A trail an auditor can walk—not a claim of perfect enforcement.

Public education totals mean something only if random names could survive a check.

Discipline is quality when money is thin.

21

Ledger

Totals allowed to move

Raised, deployed, and slices share one engine across pages.

Not a March PDF frozen for the year. Inflows and real gifts feed the same raised figure.

Education and Transparency a week apart should drift together.

Deployed, admin, and buffer sum to 100% of raised.

Named donor and simulated gift move the bar the same way—only the tag differs.

22

Maintenance

Boring repairs win

Third seal changes mean ownership survived.

A log with multiple repair lines outranks a second inauguration.

Goma’s spare shelf shortens downtime for ordinary parts.

Public packs list inspection dates beside install dates.

Maintenance culture is progress you can hold.

23

Hand-over

Keys, rota, next visit dated

Stepping back without collapse is part of done.

Completion includes who holds the key Monday and when the sample visit is booked.

Failed mid-year checks are written without cosmetics.

Success photography will dislike this standard; operators who need working stalls will not.

Progress includes the foundation’s exit.
